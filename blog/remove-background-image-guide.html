Why background removal matters

A cluttered background competes with the subject. Removing it instantly makes a product, portrait, or screenshot look more professional. Use cases include ecommerce listings, YouTube thumbnails, profile photos, presentation slides, and social graphics.

The hard part has always been doing it without expensive software or sending your file to a remote service. Browser-based removal solves both problems.

The privacy problem with upload-based tools

Most background removers work by uploading your image to a cloud API. That is fine for stock photos, but risky for product prototypes, personal photos, client work, or anything confidential. Once the file leaves your machine, you depend on someone else's privacy policy and retention schedule.

Rule: If you wouldn't post the image publicly, don't upload it to a free online tool.

How browser-based removal works

Glint AI's tool uses @imgly/background-removal, a machine-learning model compiled to WebAssembly. The model downloads once to your browser, then runs locally for every image after that. The result is a PNG with a transparent background, ready to drop into a design tool or website.

How to use the Background Remover

1
Open the tool and select your image (JPG or PNG work best).
2
Wait for the model to load on the first run. It downloads ~40MB and caches for future use.
3
Preview the cutout. The tool shows the subject on a transparent checkered background.
4
Download the PNG and use it in Canva, Figma, a website, or anywhere else.

Getting the cleanest cutout

  • Use good lighting. A clear contrast between subject and background helps the model.
  • Avoid busy edges. Fine hair, fur, and transparent objects are harder to separate cleanly.
  • Export as PNG. JPG cannot store transparency, so always download PNG.
  • Touch up afterward. For critical work, bring the result into a design tool to clean small edge pixels.

When this beats Photoshop

For quick social posts, ecommerce thumbnails, and profile pictures, browser-based removal is faster and free. For complex composites, hair masking, or print work, Photoshop still wins. Use the right tool for the job, and keep this one bookmarked for the 90% of cases that don't need layers and masks.
