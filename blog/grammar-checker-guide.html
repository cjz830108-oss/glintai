Grammar Checking That Actually Catches the Mistakes That Matter

Updated Aug 10, 2026 · 7 min read · by Glint AI

Grammar checkers can flag hundreds of issues, but only a handful actually change how readers see you. A misplaced apostrophe or a "your" where "you're" belongs undermines trust faster than a split infinitive ever will. Here's a lightweight, free Grammar Checker that focuses on the mistakes people notice — plus when to escalate to a real editor.

The mistakes that actually matter

Not all grammar errors are equal. Readers forgive an unusual sentence structure if the meaning is clear. They do not forgive errors that look careless or change meaning. The highest-impact mistakes are:

  • Homophone confusion: its/it's, your/you're, their/there/they're, then/than, affect/effect.
  • Repeated words: "the the" or "and and" slip past spellcheck but look sloppy.
  • Double spaces: Tiny, but they signal lack of polish.
  • Sentence-case errors: Starting a sentence with a lowercase letter breaks flow.
  • AI-sounding phrasing: Phrases like "delve into," "leverage," and "seamless" are not grammar errors, but they erode trust if overused.

Why over-checking hurts

Advanced grammar tools love to suggest changes that make writing more formal but less human. They flatten voice, kill rhythm, and turn a conversational blog post into a legal brief. The best editing workflow is: catch the obvious errors first, then protect the voice.

Rule: Fix errors that break trust. Ignore suggestions that break your voice.
