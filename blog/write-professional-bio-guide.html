How to Write a Professional Bio That Gets You Noticed

Updated Aug 10, 2026 · 7 min read · by Glint AI Editorial Team

Most professional bios are forgettable because they try to summarize everything. The best ones answer three questions in three lines: what you do, why it matters, and what you're after. Whether you need a LinkedIn summary, a Twitter bio, or a one-line resume pitch, the same formula works. Here it is, with templates and Glint AI's Bio & Resume Generator.

Key takeaways

  • The three-line bio formula: who you help, how you help, proof or a hook.
  • Platform differences: LinkedIn wants first-person, speaker pages want third-person.
  • Common bio mistakes: all credentials no value, jargon, no clear audience.
  • Use templates as a starting point, then tailor to each platform's voice.

The three-line bio formula

A great bio is an argument, not a history. Every line should earn its place:

Line 1 — What you do: "I help [audience] [solve problem] through [your method]."
Line 2 — Proof: "Previously [role] at [company] / [X] years of experience / built [specific thing]."
Line 3 — What you're after: "Open to [collaborations, roles, conversations] in [space]."

Platform differences

PlatformToneLength
LinkedInProfessional but conversational2–4 short paragraphs
Twitter / XWitty, specific, no fluffOne line + a emoji or CTA
ResumeDirect and achievement-focusedOne strong sentence
Personal siteVoice-forward, story-led3–5 sentences

Common bio mistakes

  • Buzzword overload. "Passionate synergy-driven innovator" means nothing.
  • Third-person stiffness. Unless it's a formal speaker bio, write in first person.
  • No clear outcome. "I do marketing" is weaker than "I help SaaS companies turn trial users into paying customers."
  • Missing call to action. Tell the reader what to do next: connect, subscribe, hire.

Templates you can use today

LinkedIn Summary: "I help [audience] [outcome] through [method]. With [X] years in [field], I've [specific result]. Right now I'm focused on [current goal]. Let's connect if you're working on something similar."
Twitter Bio: "[Role] · [X]y [field] · [narrow specialty] · [emoji/CTA]"
Resume Pitch: "[Name] — [role] with [X] years of experience in [skill 1], [skill 2], and [skill 3]. Proven track record of [result]."

Frequently asked questions

How long should a professional bio be? LinkedIn: 2–4 short paragraphs. Twitter: one line. Resume: one sentence. Match the platform.

What should a professional bio include? What you do, proof of credibility, and what you're looking for next.

Should a bio be in first or third person? First person for LinkedIn, Twitter, and personal sites. Third person for formal speaker bios and press pages.

How do I make my bio stand out? Use specific outcomes instead of buzzwords, and include one detail that only you could claim.
