# How to Write a Twitter/X Bio That Gets the Right Followers

Your bio is the first thing a stranger checks before deciding to follow. It is not your resume and not a tagline — it is a tiny ad for who you are and why someone should care. A good bio attracts the right people and filters the wrong ones. Written free and with no signup, it is a two-minute upgrade that pays back every time your profile is visited. This guide shows the shape, the edits that matter, and how to keep it honest as you grow.

## What a Twitter/X bio does

It tells visitors who you are, what you talk about, and what they get by following. In 160 characters it sets expectations. A clear bio earns the right follow; a vague one earns none or the wrong ones. It is also the line people screenshot when they introduce you, so it doubles as your one-line identity.

## When to rewrite your bio

- **A new niche** where your old bio no longer fits.
- **A follower plateau** that hints your pitch is unclear.
- **A rebrand** or career change.
- **Before a launch** when profile traffic spikes.
- **Every few months** as your work evolves.
- **After a viral post** that sends strangers to your profile.

## How to write a good bio

Lead with what you do and who it helps, add one proof or personality line, and end with a reason to follow. Skip clichés like "passionate about" and "thought leader." Use the few characters for specifics, not adjectives. Because Glint's helpers run in your browser, you can draft bios without uploading your profile anywhere.

## A step-by-step method

1. **Name your role** in plain words a stranger understands.
2. **Say who it helps** or what topic you own.
3. **Add one proof** — a result, a number, a project.
4. **Give a reason to follow** — a promise or a rhythm.
5. **Trim to fit** under 160 characters without losing the hook.

## Common mistakes

Vague identity is the main one — "just a guy who likes tech" tells nobody anything. Another is stuffing keywords with no voice, which reads as spam. Finally, never updating the bio lets it drift from what you actually post, confusing new visitors, and pasting your profile into a web generator leaks data you did not need to share.

## Who should use it (and who shouldn't)

Write a clear bio if you post publicly and want followers. Skip overthinking if your account is private and personal — there, a line to friends is enough. For any public presence, the bio pays for itself.
